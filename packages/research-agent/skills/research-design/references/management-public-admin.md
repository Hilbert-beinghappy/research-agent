# Management and Public-Administration Design Lens

Use this lens to ask better questions, not to force a preferred theory or method.

## Scope

Name the actor, institutional setting, level of analysis, decision or service process, jurisdiction, population, and time boundary. Keep individual attitudes, street-level behavior, organizational practice, interorganizational governance, and policy-system outcomes separate unless the design links them explicitly.

## Concepts and Relations

For each construct, distinguish the theoretical definition from its observed indicator. Common lenses include discretion, accountability, legitimacy, trust, administrative burden, organizational capacity, implementation, collaborative governance, procurement, and public value. These labels are prompts for definition and evidence checks; they are not established explanations by default.

A hypothesis or proposition should state its focal relation, mechanism, competing explanation, and boundary. Do not convert an institutional narrative into an individual-level effect or an observed association into a policy effect.

## Method Fit

- Descriptive questions need a defined population, denominator, measurement rule, and coverage account.
- Associational questions need a declared comparison, covariates or rival explanations, and non-causal wording.
- Causal questions need an estimand, treatment or intervention, comparison logic, timing, identification assumptions, diagnostics, and a rule that blocks causal wording when those assumptions fail.
- Interpretive questions need a defined meaning-making target, participant or material selection, positionality/reflexivity notes, and an audit trail.
- Comparative case questions need explicit selection dimensions, within-case evidence, common questions across cases, and limits on generalization.
- Process-tracing claims need temporal order, predicted mechanism observations, rival-explanation tests, source triangulation, and case-bounded conclusions.

## Ethics and Feasibility

Flag human-subject contact, employment vulnerability, sensitive administrative data, protected groups, proprietary vendor material, small-cell disclosure, organizational access, and retention obligations. Record what review or permission is required; never describe the checklist as approval.
